## Findings
- Over 250 GW of gas-fired generating capacity was in development in the US as of 2025, with ~1/3 slated to power data centers on-site; 2026 could add >100 GW if all planned projects start — exceeding the 2002 shale-boom record. [1]
- US data center electricity consumption projected to increase by ~240 TWh (+130%) by 2030 vs 2024 per IEA Base Case; global DC demand reaches ~945 TWh by 2030 (~3% of global electricity). [3]
- Goldman Sachs forecasts a 165% increase in data center power demand by 2030; S&P Global sees US DC grid power demand rising 22% in 2025 alone and nearly tripling by 2030. [4][5]
- GE Vernova ending 2025 with ~80 GW gas turbine backlog stretching into 2029; expects to be sold out through 2030 by end-2026. Guiding to 20 GW annualized production by mid-2026 and up to 24 GW by mid-2028. [6]
- US gas turbine lead times have stretched to 5–7 years for large-frame units (vs ~2 years in 2021); turbine pricing up sharply. Siemens Energy committed $1B and GE Vernova $600M in US manufacturing expansion. [6][7]
- Texas alone accounts for ~80.6 GW of planned US gas capacity — nearly a third of the national pipeline and a ~4x increase YoY. [1]
- Argan Inc. reported RECORD $3.0B consolidated backlog as of Oct 31, 2025 (vs $1.4B at Jan 31, 2025 — up ~114%); gas-fired plants = ~79% of backlog. Under contract for ~6 GW of generation, including two new Texas projects (1.4 GW CPV Basin Ranch + 860 MW). [9][10]
- TerraPower Natrium (345 MWe sodium-cooled) is first US commercial SMR under construction in Wyoming, targeting ~2030 operation; $3.4B funded ($2B DOE + $1.4B private incl. NVIDIA/HD Hyundai $650M). NRC SMR licensing decisions expected through 2026 — meaningful SMR capacity unlikely before late-decade. [11][12]
- LNG EPC pipeline is massive: Rio Grande LNG Trains 4-5 awarded ~$9B to Bechtel (Train 4 alone ~$4.3B LSTK); Woodside Louisiana LNG 3-train 16.5 MTPA foundation awarded to Bechtel; Port Arthur LNG Trains 1-2 starting 2027-2028. Data centers expected to raise US gas production needs 10-15% over 5 years. [13][1]
- OBBBA (July 2025) terminated key IRA ITC provisions for solar/wind: projects must begin construction by July 4, 2026 or be placed in service by Dec 31, 2027; FEOC content restrictions take effect 2026 — near-term renewable EPC rush followed by likely 2027+ slowdown, reinforcing gas dominance. [14]

## Deep Read Notes

### Source [1]: Global Energy Monitor — US gas power development report
Key data: 250+ GW US gas pipeline in 2025 (1/3 for data centers direct); 2026 could add 100+ GW (vs 2002 record ~100 GW). 2025 construction additions likely >10 GW (equal to NYC peak demand). Texas 80.6 GW ~= 33% of national pipeline, +4x YoY. DC demand to raise US nat gas production 10-15% over 5 years. 1,000+ data centers coming online over 5 years with 75 GW capacity.
Key insight: The US is now the global leader in new gas power development, specifically because of data center colocation. 3x proposal growth YoY in 2025.
Useful for: Industry tailwind thesis, Argan demand backdrop, Texas concentration (Argan builds in Texas).

### Source [3][4][5]: IEA / Goldman Sachs / S&P Global — Data center demand
Key data: IEA Base Case global DC demand ~945 TWh by 2030 (+240 TWh US alone, +130%); Goldman 165% growth by 2030; S&P +22% in 2025 and nearly 3x by 2030; Gartner 448 TWh (2025) -> 980 TWh (2030). AI-optimized servers: 93 TWh (2025) -> 432 TWh (2030), nearly 5x.
Key insight: Base case projections from top-tier sources all converge on ~doubling by 2030. US + China = ~80% of growth.
Useful for: Macro TAM sizing, demand justification for gas buildout.

### Source [6][7][8]: Gas turbine supply — GE Vernova / Siemens / Mitsubishi
Key data: GE Vernova 80 GW backlog end-2025 → sold out through 2030 by end-2026. Production: 20 GW annualized by mid-2026, 24 GW by mid-2028. Lead times 5-7 years (vs 2 yrs in 2021). Siemens Energy $1B US manufacturing investment; GE Vernova $600M. Turbine prices up sharply.
Key insight: Supply constraint is the bottleneck — creates pricing power for EPC firms who already secured turbines (Argan's $3B backlog implies they locked in slots). New entrants are effectively frozen out until 2030+.
Useful for: Argan competitive moat section, explaining why Argan's backlog converted rapidly.

### Source [9][10]: Argan Q3 FY2026 results
Key data: $3.0B backlog Oct 31, 2025 vs $1.4B Jan 31, 2025 (+114%). Gas-fired ~79% of mix. ~6 GW under contract. New wins: 1.4 GW CPV Basin Ranch (Ward County TX) + 860 MW TX project. APC subsidiary won SSE Thermal Platin Power Station Ireland (3x Siemens SGT-800, 170 MW, complete 2028).
Key insight: Backlog more than doubled in 9 months — direct evidence of the tailwind converting to contracted revenue. Texas exposure aligns with national pipeline concentration.
Useful for: Direct Argan financial tie-in, backlog growth chart, revenue visibility.

### Source [11][12]: SMR outlook
Key data: TerraPower Natrium 345 MWe under construction Wyoming, demonstration ~2030. NRC environmental review Oct 2025, safety eval Dec 2025, permit decision H1 2026. $3.4B funding ($2B DOE + $1.4B private, incl. NVIDIA/HD Hyundai $650M). NuScale: Idaho pilot scrapped; TVA/ENTRA1 MOU for up to 6 GW; 12 modules in production at Doosan. NRC SMR licensing decisions expected 2026.
Key insight: SMRs are a 2030s story, not 2026-2028. Near-term, gas fills the gap. Hyperscaler SMR PPAs announced but construction not imminent.
Useful for: Contextualizing SMR as complement (not substitute) to gas in near term.

### Source [13]: LNG export EPC pipeline
Key data: Rio Grande LNG Train 4 ~$4.3B LSTK to Bechtel; Trains 4+5 total ~$9B. Woodside Louisiana LNG 3-train 16.5 MTPA (revised LSTK Bechtel deal, NTP Dec 2024). Port Arthur LNG Trains 1+2 operational 2027/2028. Multi-year EPC backlogs for Bechtel (Argan's larger EPC peer).
Key insight: LNG EPC market is dominated by Bechtel/Worley at mega-project scale; Argan plays downstream in gas-fired generation, which is a different but correlated market (gas demand → more gas production → lower fuel cost → cheaper gas generation).
Useful for: Cross-referencing gas value chain buildout; Argan is not a direct LNG EPC player but benefits from cheap feedstock narrative.

### Source [14]: IRA → OBBBA tax credit impact
Key data: July 2025 OBBBA terminated ITC for new solar/wind unless (a) construction begins by July 4, 2026, or (b) placed in service by Dec 31, 2027. FEOC restrictions on Chinese/Russian/Iranian/NK content begin 2026. Residential clean energy 30% credit ended Dec 31, 2025.
Key insight: Near-term pull-forward boom for renewable EPC (developers racing July 2026 construction deadline), then expected cliff in 2027+ — which further amplifies gas-fired demand as the only dispatchable scale option.
Useful for: IRA impact section, explaining renewables are decelerating just as gas accelerates.
