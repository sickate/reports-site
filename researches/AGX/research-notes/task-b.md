## Findings

- Q4 FY2026 revenue was $262.1M, up ~13% YoY; slightly below ~$271M analyst consensus. [2][3]
- FY2026 net income reached $137.8M ($9.74 diluted EPS), a 62% YoY jump from $85M ($6.15 EPS) in FY2025. [1][3]
- Q4 FY2026 net income was $49M vs $31M prior-year quarter; diluted EPS $3.47 vs $2.22 (+57% YoY), beating $2.13 consensus. [2][3]
- FY2026 gross margin expanded to 20.5% from 16.1% in FY2025; Q4 gross margin was 25.0%. EBITDA reached $162.8M (17.2% margin, up from 13.0%). Operating income ~$134.7M (~14.3% operating margin). [1][2]
- Consolidated project backlog at Jan 31, 2026 was ~$2.9B, roughly doubling from ~$1.4B at Jan 31, 2025. During FY2026 the company added $2.5B in new contract value. Mix: 77% natural gas, 14% renewables, 9% industrial. Power segment backlog ~$2.7B. [1][2][3]
- Peak intra-year backlog hit a record $3.0B at end of Q3 FY2026 (Oct 31, 2025) after two new Texas gas projects (1.4 GW CPV Basin Ranch + 860 MW); total under contract ~6 GW of power generation. - Power segment produced $756.5M (80.1%) of FY2026 revenue; Q4 Power segment revenue ~$204M (78%). [2]

- Capital return: quarterly dividend raised 33% from $0.375 to $0.50/share in Dec 2025 (3rd consecutive annual hike, $2.00 annualized); on Apr 8, 2026 the Board increased share repurchase authorization from $150M to $200M and extended through Jan 31, 2030; declared another $0.50 quarterly dividend payable Apr 30, 2026. ### Source [4][5]: Argan Q3 FY2026 (quarter ended Oct 31, 2025) — Business Wire + StockTitan
Key data:
- Q3 FY2026 revenue: $251.2M, down 2.3% YoY (timing/project-mix effect; large prior-year projects in peak execution vs new awards in early construction).
- Q3 gross profit $46.9M, gross margin 18.7%.
- Q3 net income $30.7M, diluted EPS $2.17 (beat consensus), EBITDA $40.3M.
- Peak project backlog $3.0B — record — after adding two Texas gas-fired projects (1.4 GW CPV Basin Ranch + 860 MW); cumulative ~6 GW of power generating assets under contract.
- Dividend raised 33% from $0.375 to $0.50/quarter (3rd consecutive annual increase), $2.00 annualized.
Key insight: The Q3→Q4 backlog step-down from $3.0B to $2.9B reflects $262M of Q4 revenue burn partially offset by continued bookings. The apparent Q3 revenue dip is a transition artifact, not demand weakness — backlog accelerated even as recent large projects finished. Management is comfortable enough with cash generation to raise dividend mid-year.
Useful for: sequential trend narrative, demonstrating backlog quality and power-generation exposure (gas-heavy, AI/data-center-linked).
