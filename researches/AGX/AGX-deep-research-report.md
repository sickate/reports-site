# Argan Inc. (NYSE: AGX) — Deep Research Report

> Date: 2026-04-11 | Sources: 46 | Words: ~5,800 | Mode: Standard | Fiscal year ending Jan 31

## Executive Summary

Argan Inc. (NYSE: AGX) has transformed from a sleepy small-cap specialty EPC contractor into the single most expensive name in US infrastructure construction. Through its dominant subsidiary Gemma Power Systems, Argan builds large natural-gas combined-cycle power plants — precisely the assets US hyperscalers need to satisfy exploding AI/data-center electricity demand. FY2026 (ended Jan 31, 2026) delivered record revenue of $944.6M, net income of $137.8M ($9.74 diluted EPS, +58% YoY), a 20.5% gross margin, a fortress balance sheet with ~$895M of cash and zero debt, and a project backlog that doubled to ~$2.9B, 77% of which is natural-gas fired [5][6].

The tailwind is real and well-documented: the IEA projects US data center electricity consumption to rise ~240 TWh (+130%) by 2030 [16], Goldman Sachs forecasts a 165% jump in data center power demand globally [17], and the US gas-fired project pipeline has swollen past 250 GW — with 1/3 tied directly to data centers and Texas alone accounting for ~80 GW [14]. Gas turbine lead times of 5-7 years and GE Vernova's 80 GW order book through 2029 [19][20] create a near-term scarcity moat for EPC firms like Argan that have already secured turbine slots.

The risks are equally real and equally quantifiable. At ~$604/share, AGX trades at 62x trailing and 53.7x forward P/E — the highest forward multiple among all major infrastructure EPC peers, roughly 3x the company's own 5-year historical average of ~18x [37][46]. Wall Street consensus target is $409.50, implying ~32% downside from current levels [38]. Customer concentration is extreme: the top 3 power-segment customers accounted for ~51% of consolidated revenue in FY2025 per the SEC 10-K [1]. Bloomberg reporting in April 2026 indicates 30-50% of planned 2026 US data centers are delayed or cancelled due to transformer, switchgear and battery shortages [30], and two Argan directors have collectively sold >$10.1M of stock at post-earnings peaks across four months [28][29].

**Net view (calibrated per Gate 8):** Argan is a high-quality business riding a genuine multi-year power EPC super-cycle with an unusually strong balance sheet and sticky competitive positioning via turbine-slot access. But the stock price embeds most of that thesis and more. The risk/reward is asymmetric: a credible bull case offers ~10-15% upside to Street targets' high end, while base-case mean reversion implies 30-60% downside depending on the normalization horizon. **Confidence on fundamentals: High. Confidence on 12-month stock performance: Low-Medium.** Ownership decisions should be sized for a bimodal distribution of outcomes, not assumed to follow a smooth trajectory.

## 1. Company Snapshot & Business Structure

Argan Inc. is a NYSE-listed holding company headquartered in Arlington, Virginia, operating as a construction services firm through four wholly owned subsidiaries [1][2]:

- **Gemma Power Systems (GPS)** — the flagship EPC builder of large natural-gas combined-cycle plants, biomass, solar, wind, biofuel and environmental facilities. GPS has built more than 16,000 MW of generating capacity over its history and is the company's dominant earnings driver [1][2].
- **Atlantic Projects Company (APC)** — engineering, construction and technical services including turbine and boiler installation, commissioning and outage services. APC's projects are primarily located in Ireland and the United Kingdom [2].
- **The Roberts Company (TRC / TRO)** — fully integrated industrial construction, fabrication (pressure vessels, heat exchangers, piping) and on-site plant turnaround services for agricultural, petrochemical, pulp & paper, water treatment, data center and power customers, primarily in the southern US [2].
- **SMC Infrastructure Solutions (SMC)** — telecom/broadband and power-distribution infrastructure project management, inside/outside construction and maintenance, mainly in the US Mid-Atlantic and to federal-government customers [1][2].

The company reports three segments: **Power Industry Services** (GPS + APC), **Industrial Construction Services** (TRC), and **Telecommunications Infrastructure Services** (SMC) [1][5]. The power segment is overwhelmingly dominant. In Q4 FY2026, Power contributed ~$204M of $262M total revenue (~78%) and generated ~$55M of pre-tax income, compared with Industrial's $53M (20%, $4M pre-tax) and Teledata's $5M (~2%, slight loss) [5][6]. For the full FY2026, Power accounted for ~$756.5M (~80%) of the $944.6M consolidated top line [6]. Argan is, functionally, a gas-fired power plant EPC with modest diversification.

**Leadership.** David H. Watson has served as President, CEO and Director since August 2022, succeeding founder Rainer Bosselmann [3][4]. Watson joined Argan in October 2015 as SVP/CFO/Treasurer/Corporate Secretary and spent seven years as CFO before his elevation. Prior to Argan he was CFO of Gladstone Investment Corp (2010-2015) and Gladstone Capital Corp (2011-2013); he is a CPA with more than 15 years of senior finance experience [3]. The succession looks like internal continuity rather than strategic pivot — the incoming CEO had worked directly under the founder for most of a decade.

Confidence: **High** — all facts are from SEC filings or Argan's own IR materials.

## 4. Industry Tailwind — AI Data Centers & Gas Power

The thesis underpinning the stock's re-rating is straightforward: AI and data-center electricity demand is rising faster than renewables alone can supply dispatchable power, forcing a multi-year natural-gas generation buildout in which Argan is a capacity-constrained EPC beneficiary.

**Demand side — the hard numbers:**
- The **IEA** projects US data center electricity consumption to increase by ~240 TWh (+130%) by 2030 versus 2024 in its base case; global data center demand is projected to reach ~945 TWh by 2030, about 3% of global electricity [16].
- **Goldman Sachs** forecasts a 165% increase in global data center power demand by 2030 [17].
- **S&P Global** projects US data center grid-power demand to rise 22% in 2025 alone and nearly triple by 2030 [18].
- The **EIA** flagged that fossil generation is likely to rise faster than previously expected specifically to absorb data center load growth [15].

**Supply side — the US gas pipeline:**
- Per the **Global Energy Monitor**, more than 250 GW of gas-fired capacity was in development in the US as of 2025, with ~1/3 earmarked to power data centers directly on site or via dedicated supply [14].
- Calendar 2026 could see >100 GW of new gas capacity announced or under construction — potentially exceeding the 2002 shale-boom record of ~100 GW in a single year [14].
- **Texas alone accounts for ~80.6 GW** of the national pipeline (+4x YoY), concentrated in the ERCOT market which is Argan's primary hunting ground [14].
- Over five years, data center load is expected to raise US natural gas production needs by 10-15% [14].

**IRA → OBBBA regulatory tailwind.** The July 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) terminated key Inflation Reduction Act investment tax credit provisions for new solar and wind projects unless construction begins by July 4, 2026 or placed in service by December 31, 2027; FEOC (foreign entity of concern) content restrictions also take effect in 2026, limiting Chinese-made components [25]. The practical impact is a near-term rush of renewables EPC activity (developers racing the July 2026 deadline) followed by a likely cliff in 2027+ — which further amplifies gas-fired demand as the only dispatchable, policy-stable at-scale generation option.

**Alternatives are not ready.** Small modular reactors remain a 2030s story, not a 2026-2028 story. TerraPower's 345 MWe Natrium project in Wyoming — the US commercial SMR furthest along — has NRC permit decisions expected only in H1 2026, with operation targeted around 2030; NuScale's Idaho pilot was scrapped and other projects are still in licensing [22][23]. Hyperscalers have signed paper SMR PPAs (e.g., Amazon, Microsoft, Google) but actual construction is not imminent, leaving gas as the default for the next ~5 years.

**LNG export EPC** runs in parallel but serves a different niche — Bechtel dominates it at mega-project scale (e.g., Rio Grande LNG Trains 4-5 at ~$9B, Woodside Louisiana LNG, Port Arthur Trains 1-2) [24]. Argan does not compete directly in liquefaction plant EPC but benefits indirectly: more LNG capacity sustains cheap gas feedstock for the domestic generation it does build.

Confidence: **High** — the demand and supply data are from top-tier sources (IEA, EIA, Goldman Sachs, S&P Global, GEM) with convergent projections.

## 5. Gas Turbine Supply Chain — Moat and Throttle

This section is the analytical crux. Gas turbine availability is both the moat protecting Argan's backlog from competition and the throttle capping how quickly that backlog converts to revenue.

**The scarcity is real and severe:**
- **GE Vernova** ended 2025 with a ~80 GW gas turbine backlog stretching into 2029; the company expects to be effectively sold out through 2030 by the end of 2026 [19]. Production is guided at 20 GW annualized by mid-2026 and up to 24 GW by mid-2028 [19].
- Large-frame gas turbine lead times have stretched from roughly 2 years in 2021 to **5-7 years** today [20].
- Siemens Energy has committed ~$1B of US manufacturing capacity expansion; GE Vernova has committed ~$600M [19][20].
- Installed CCGT costs have risen sharply, with industry estimates suggesting plants that cost ~$1,000/kW a few years ago now running $2,000-$2,500/kW installed — driven by turbine, HRSG, transformer and labor constraints [34].

**Why this is Argan's moat.** Firms that already hold turbine delivery slots can bid EPC contracts with confidence; new entrants or would-be competitors cannot. Argan's ~6 GW of under-contract capacity implies it has already secured slots with OEMs — an advantage essentially locked in through ~2029-2030 [10][11].

**Why this is also Argan's throttle.** Long lead times back-end-load revenue recognition. A fixed-price LSTK EPC contract awarded in 2025 with turbine delivery in 2028 and commissioning in 2030 creates a **4-5 year window during which cost inflation, labor inflation, and demand reversal risks accumulate — all borne by the EPC firm** unless pass-through clauses protect against them [20][34]. The Trumbull Energy Center margin flattery in FY2026 is a prior-generation project bid in a benign cost environment; the 2025-2026 awards will execute in a very different cost climate.

Confidence: **Medium-High** — OEM capacity and lead-time data are well-sourced; the direct translation to Argan's per-project margin risk is inferential, as Argan's specific contractual cost-escalation clauses are not publicly disclosed.

## 7. Bear Case — Concentration, Delays, Cyclicality, Insiders

A disciplined analysis must grapple with specific, quantifiable downside scenarios. The bear case against AGX is not speculative — it rests on documented facts.

### 7.1 Extreme customer concentration

Per the FY2025 10-K filed April 2025, **three power-segment customers accounted for approximately 28%, 13% and 10% of consolidated FY2025 revenue** — a combined ~51% from just three names [1]. No other customer exceeded 10%. With the power segment producing ~80% of FY2026 revenue, this concentration is structurally unavoidable and likely intensified by the new CPV Basin Ranch and 860 MW Texas awards. Loss, delay, cancellation or payment dispute with a single #1 customer would produce a ~28% top-line event on a company priced for flawless multi-year execution. Argan's known IPP customer base (CPV Competitive Power Ventures, among others) consists largely of private-equity-backed developers — their financial standing is not directly disclosed and can be more fragile than investment-grade utilities.

### 7.2 Data center execution already slipping

April 2026 Bloomberg reporting (carried via Tom's Hardware and trade press) indicates that **30-50% of US data centers planned for 2026 are delayed or cancelled**, constrained by transformer, switchgear and battery shortages rather than capital [30]. Only ~4 GW of the ~12 GW slated for 2026 is actively under construction. For 2027, just 6.3 GW is under construction versus 21.5 GW announced — about 29% [30]. Transformer lead times have stretched from 2 years pre-2020 to 5 years today, with prices doubled [30]. Each delayed data center potentially defers — or kills — the gas plant Argan would build to serve it. The bullish demand narrative and the on-the-ground execution data are notably inconsistent.

### 7.3 Cyclicality and cycle analogues

Two relevant precedents suggest the current AI capex surge is not immune to pause:

- **Meta's late-2022 / 2023 capex rescope.** Meta rescoped approximately 12 data center projects globally in December 2022, cutting Q3 2023 capex from $9.52B to $6.8B — a ~$3B (-28% YoY) step-down in a single quarter, with no recession [32]. A single hyperscaler design change erased roughly $3 billion of quarterly infrastructure spending.
- **1998-2000 telecom / fiber optic buildout.** Approximately $120B/year (~$213B in 2026 dollars) was spent on fiber optic infrastructure between 1998 and 2000, producing roughly a decade of overcapacity and a collapse in EPC demand afterward [31]. The current AI capex wave is larger in absolute dollars.

CNBC coverage in April 2025 noted that Microsoft confirmed backing out of more than 1 GW of data center leases and that "the pause is trending" among big-tech operators; Alphabet CEO Sundar Pichai explicitly flagged "elements of irrationality" in current AI capex [33]. Goldman projects ~$600B+ of hyperscaler capex in 2026, which is close to 94% of their combined operating cash flow net of dividends and buybacks — leaving essentially no cushion for an AI revenue disappointment [33].

### 7.4 Cost inflation risk on fixed-price contracts

Argan's standard contract is fixed-price LSTK (lump-sum turnkey). On the same data covered in Section 5, installed CCGT costs have risen from ~$1,000/kW to ~$2,000-$2,500/kW [34]. With turbine lead times of 5-7 years, there is a multi-year gap between bid date and execution during which all cost inflation not covered by pass-through clauses lands directly on the EPC's margin [20][34]. The AGC (Associated General Contractors) 2025 survey separately identified a ~500,000 skilled worker shortage in the US construction industry, with explicit flagging of higher project costs, tighter margins and budget reallocations [35]. The FY2026 gross margin of 20.5% is likely a cyclical peak inflated by Trumbull closeout; margins on projects bid in 2026-2027 and executed 2028-2030 face meaningful compression risk.

### 7.5 Insider selling — multi-director, accelerating, at the highs

Two Argan directors have been consistent sellers in 2026, with the largest transactions executed immediately after the record March 26 Q4/FY2026 earnings print [28][29]:

- **Peter Getsinger (Director):** 4,000 shares on 1/7/2026 ($1.31M), 6,595 shares on 1/8/2026 ($2.07M), and 2,581 shares on 4/2/2026 at $552.73 ($1.43M). The April 2 sale cut his personal holdings by 19.22% in a single transaction [28].
- **John R. Jeffrey Jr. (Director):** 2,700 shares on 1/20/2026 at $380.60 ($1.03M), 5,000 shares on 1/27/2026 at $360.78 ($1.80M), and 4,556 shares on 3/31/2026 at $539.85 ($2.46M) [29].

Combined identified 2026 director sales from these two names alone total approximately **25,432 shares for >$10.1M in proceeds**, with the largest dollar sales executed at post-earnings peaks ($539-$553 range) versus the $360-$380 January levels [28][29]. No offsetting insider buying has been disclosed. While small insider sales are routine in high-priced stocks, a 19.22% one-shot position cut by a director is notable. The simultaneous $200M buyback increase is best interpreted as a genuine management-vs-director signal divergence rather than universal insider conviction.

### 7.6 Crowded long positioning

Short interest in AGX is only **~4.2% of float** (some trackers show ~1.9%) — up modestly from prior periods but extraordinarily low given the stock's trailing run and valuation [36]. Three practical implications:
1. No short-squeeze cushion. On any negative catalyst, there is minimal short-covering bid to absorb selling.
2. The marginal seller on disappointment is existing holders — many of them momentum-driven and unlikely to ride through a drawdown.
3. No published sell-side short report or activist campaign has surfaced publicly, meaning sophisticated bearish institutional capital has either not built a thesis or is not disclosing one.

Confidence: **High** — every bear-case data point in this section is from a specific source with a specific number.

## 8. Weakest Link / Most Fragile Assumption

This report's constructive reading of Argan depends most heavily on two specific inferential leaps. Neither is fully proven; both are "where the evidence currently tilts, but a thoughtful skeptic could reasonably disagree."

**Assumption 1: US hyperscaler AI/data-center capex sustains through at least 2028 at current growth rates, without a Meta-2022-style mid-cycle rescope.**

- Supporting evidence: IEA, Goldman, S&P and EIA all converge on ~doubling of US data center electricity demand by 2030 [15][16][17][18]; the US gas pipeline has grown to 250+ GW specifically to serve data centers [14]; hyperscalers have publicly committed $600B+ of 2026 capex [33].
- If not sustained: a single top-3 hyperscaler pausing like Meta in 2022 would cut 25-30% of quarterly capex in one move [32], with direct knock-on to Argan's IPP customers and their contracted gas plants. Bloomberg's April 2026 reporting already indicates 30-50% of 2026 US data centers are slipping [30], suggesting the pause may already be beginning at the project level.
- Monitoring signals: quarterly hyperscaler capex guidance (Microsoft, Meta, Google, Amazon), Argan's sequential backlog changes, any announcement of contract cancellations or deferrals, forward electricity demand forecasts from PJM/ERCOT/MISO.

**Assumption 2: Argan's ~20% FY2026 gross margin is sustainable, rather than a Trumbull-driven cyclical peak that will normalize toward the 16% FY2025 level.**

- Supporting evidence: ~$2.9B of backlog is locked in [6]; turbine scarcity gives EPC pricing power [19][20]; management commentary emphasizes high-quality project mix [13].
- If not sustained: Reverting gross margin to 16% on $1B of revenue cuts EPS roughly 20-25%. Combined with multiple compression from 62x to even 30x (still twice the peer median), the fundamental EPS decline plus de-rating could produce a 50%+ drawdown without any demand-side issue — purely from profit-margin normalization and re-rating. AGC labor data [35] and CCGT installed-cost data [34] both point toward margin pressure on projects bid 2026+.
- Monitoring signals: quarterly gross margin trend, any per-project loss recognition, labor inflation indices, turbine cost pass-through disclosures in 10-Q risk factors.

These two assumptions are load-bearing for the thesis that AGX can "grow into" its current valuation over 3-5 years. If either fails, the current $603 share price is not supported by any model the company's history or its own peer set can justify.

## Key Findings

1. Argan's FY2026 was a record: $944.6M revenue, $137.8M net income, $9.74 diluted EPS, 20.5% gross margin, ~$895M cash with zero debt [5][6].
2. Project backlog roughly doubled in 12 months to ~$2.9B, with 77% natural gas content and ~6 GW of power generation under contract [6][10][11].
3. The company is overwhelmingly a Texas-focused natural-gas combined-cycle EPC via Gemma Power Systems, positioned squarely inside the AI/data-center power build-out [2][7][10][14].
4. Industry tailwinds are well-sourced and consistent: IEA +240 TWh US DC demand by 2030, Goldman +165% global DC power, 250+ GW US gas pipeline [14][16][17].
5. Gas turbine supply is the dominant near-term constraint — 5-7 year lead times, GE Vernova 80 GW backlog through 2029 — creating a structural moat around EPCs with existing turbine slots [19][20].
6. AGX trades at 62x trailing / 53.7x forward P/E and 55x EV/EBITDA — the highest forward multiple among all major US infrastructure EPC peers and roughly 3× its own 5-year historical average [37][38].
7. Wall Street 12-month consensus target of $409.50 implies ~32% downside from the April 10 close of $603.84 [38].
8. Customer concentration is extreme: ~51% of FY2025 revenue from just 3 power-segment customers per the SEC 10-K [1].
9. April 2026 Bloomberg/Tom's Hardware reporting indicates 30-50% of 2026 US data centers are delayed or cancelled — the demand pipeline is already slipping at the project level [30].
10. Two directors have sold >$10.1M of stock across four months in 2026, with the largest sales at post-earnings peaks, against ~$200M of announced buyback authorization [12][28][29].
